The science behind Rikudo³ ↗
  • Fill every cell with 1 → N so that each number touches the next.
  • A gold chute joins two cells that hold consecutive numbers. Once the path enters a chute, it has to go through.
  • Given numbers are fixed. The path starts at the flag (1) and ends at home, the gold house. There is exactly one solution.
  • Drag to rotate. On the cube, double-tap the background to straighten it square-on. Tap or click an empty cell to place the next number. Tap a number to build on from it, and tap it again to flip between building up (+) and down (−). To place any number anywhere, pick it with ‹ › in the Puzzle menu.
  • Erase a cell: long-press it (or right-click on desktop).
  • Underlined numbers (6, 9, 66, 16…) are the ones that would read as another number upside down.
  • Red means a number breaks a rule right now, and Rule check in the Puzzle menu says which one. A number can also be wrong without breaking a rule yet.
  • Hint fixes one wrong number, otherwise it reveals the next one. Hold Hint (or Shift-click it) to peek at the solution for 3 s. Check erases every wrong number. Each costs a share of your score, even after Restart.
  • Time: tap it to pause. Restart: tap twice to confirm, hold for a new puzzle.
  • Keys: H hint · Shift+H peek · C check · P pause · Ctrl+Z undo · +/− direction · digits type a number · arrows move.

Solved! That's the whole game.

  • The real boards are a cube, a sphere and a pyramid. Drag to turn them.
  • Logic is always enough: every puzzle has exactly one solution.
  • Hint places or fixes one number; hold it to peek at the solution. Check clears wrong numbers. Help costs points.
  • Tap the gold pause button by the clock to take a break. The menu has new puzzles, your scores and the full instructions.
  • Start with Beginner puzzles: they need nothing beyond what you just did. Then step up to Easy.
